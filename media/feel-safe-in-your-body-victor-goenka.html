With Victor Goenka on Feel Safe in Your Body · August 3, 2026

Watch the full episode →

Summary

On Victor Goenka's podcast Feel Safe in Your Body, Trung talks about the years before the diagnostic work — roughly a decade of pushing his output higher, chasing the next goal, and three burnouts before he recognized the pattern: chasing more goals never fixed what was actually driving him, it just gave the same old pattern more speed.

Much of the conversation traces back to long solo cycling trips through Vietnam, where the discomfort of the ride surfaced small things he'd normally push past — until they compounded. He describes learning to notice the state he's in before it turns into a decision: dysregulated, he overreaches; calm, he decides better. The tools he names are ordinary — pacing, breath, taking a break before the crash instead of after.

The part that connects directly to the commercial work: he describes his actual practice with founders as uncovering the surface story. The stated problem — "my team is too slow," "my execution is too slow" — isn't the real one, and the session exists to find what actually is. That's the same mechanism as the Founder Bottleneck Diagnostic, described here in nervous-system language instead of "constraint" language: name what's actually running you, and you can act on it.

Key questions answered

  • Why did pushing harder stop working, even though it looked successful from the outside?
  • What actually happens on a long solo bike ride that surfaces old patterns?
  • How does knowing your own state in the moment change a business decision?
  • What is the real work with founders — and how does it connect to the Founder Bottleneck Diagnostic?

In his own words

"The growth is always in the discomfort. The next edge of growth is where you feel uncomfortable."

"When you know what's actually running you — when you know the pattern — you can act on it."

"The surface symptoms are usually... my team is too slow, I cannot decide, my execution is too slow — and I help them to uncover the actual reason."
