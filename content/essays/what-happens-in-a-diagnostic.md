A Founder Bottleneck Diagnostic is one 90-minute session. You state what you think is wrong with your company. We pull the concrete facts — what you've tried, what happened, what the numbers say. We find the gap between your explanation and the evidence, name the actual pattern, and you leave with one concrete move. No report, no retainer, no follow-up program. The session is done when you have language for what to stop.

That's the whole answer. The rest of this essay is what each step looks like in practice.

## The five steps

**1. You name the stated problem.** You explain what you think is wrong, in your own words, uninterrupted. This matters more than it sounds: the language a founder uses to describe their problem is usually part of the problem.

**2. We pull the facts.** What have you actually tried? What happened when you tried it? What does the situation look like in numbers and events, rather than in feelings? This is where most sessions turn. One founder ran an AI public speaking platform and was sure his marketing was too slow. The question that broke it open was "how many landing page views do you have?" He didn't know. He had tracking running — he'd [never opened it](/case-studies/the-dashboard-he-never-opened).

**3. We find the gap.** Between your diagnosis and what the data shows there is almost always daylight. Six months of outreach producing three conversations is a gap. A "slow marketing" problem sitting on top of an unread analytics dashboard is a gap.

**4. The real pattern gets named.** One crisp formulation you couldn't produce from inside. This is where the value sits — and it's why founders keep describing the session the same way afterwards: "I already knew it, I just hadn't said it out loud." A rugby club founder put it best:

> "You know that way you already know something, but it hits when someone says it to you straight? Sometimes you just need to take your medicine."
>
> *Johny Harris, Dai Viet Rugby*

**5. One concrete move.** You leave with the one thing to stop doing and the move to make instead. Not a roadmap. Founders who get the constraint named tend to execute fast on their own — [Minh at Algomate](/case-studies/algomate-10-hours-a-week) rebuilt his outreach routine the same week.

## Why naming works when advice doesn't

You already have the information. You've been living inside the problem for months. What's missing is a version of it you can act on — because your current explanation has been keeping you comfortable, and it's usually wrong in a specific, findable way. New advice stacked on a wrong explanation just gets executed in the wrong direction.

So the session doesn't give you new information. It gives back what you told it, with the pattern made visible. Action was always available. The word for what to stop doing wasn't.

## Who it's for — and who it's not for

It's built for capable founders who are compensating for a blind spot instead of resolving it. The tell: a lot of thinking, not a lot of deciding. Smart, busy, and in the same place as six months ago. Same problem, different explanation each quarter.

Don't book one if you want validation for a decision you've made, if you're looking for ongoing coaching, or if you're at idea stage with no team or product yet. Those need different things.

## A decision rule

If the same problem has survived three different explanations, your explanations are the problem — get it diagnosed. If you can name the number that would prove your current explanation right, and you've actually looked at it, you probably don't need a session yet.
